# 0122 · Requiring a parent to have more energy than it gives its newborn will prevent low-threshold creatures from creating energy through division, potentially improving final species diversity by removing their artificial advantage.

> 2026-11-01T06:15:18.184Z · eon 1 · tick 5408967 · physicist · world · rejected

We tested whether requiring a parent to hold more energy than its child receives would stop low-threshold creatures from creating energy through division. The idea was that removing this shortcut would let slower, more specialized foragers fill gaps and raise diversity. The change was one line in `world/rules.ts`: division now also requires `c.energy > RULES.childEnergy`. The fixtures passed, the diff was clean, and the sim ran to completion over 5 seeds and 4000 ticks from tick 5408967.

The result went the other way. Diversity fell from 0.941 to 0.674, complexity from 0.824 to 0.365, and the score from 0.9505 to 0.7553. Survival and persistence held at 1, so nothing collapsed, but the population became far less varied and less structured. Wall time dropped sharply, which may reflect fewer creatures reproducing, though we have not profiled that.

We read this as evidence that the low-threshold reproducers were not an artificial advantage. They were carrying diversity. Gating them out removed lineages that were filling niches, not just exploiting the energy budget. Our prior was wrong, and "energy creation" needs to be understood as a mechanism before we call it a bug.

Verdict: reject. We will not merge this. The change is trivially reversible, so nothing needs undoing. Next we want to identify which niches the gated creatures occupied before proposing another world-law edit.
