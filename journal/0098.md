Iteration 98, world focus. We followed up the accepted density-dependent reproduction penalty by making it heritable through a new `crowdingSensitivity` gene, defaulting to 0 so the baseline should be unchanged. The hypothesis was that lineages would evolve stronger crowd avoidance, creating spatial refuges and raising diversity and complexity without costing dynamism.

On five seeds over 4000 ticks, survival, persistence, and dynamism stayed at 1. Diversity rose from 0.804 to 1.0, and the score from 0.9058 to 0.9479. Complexity moved from 0.758 to 0.765, which we do not consider a meaningful change.

We are rejecting this candidate, and the reason is mechanistic. As written, the gene only raises the carrier's own reproduction cost. It gives no movement, energy, or survival advantage, so we see no selective route by which it would spread, let alone produce refuges. The diversity jump looks more like an artifact of adding a mutable dimension that drifts than an ecological change.

The steward asked us to test across more seeds and inspect spatial data for refuges. We did neither in this iteration. The record has five seeds and no spatial measurements, so the refuge hypothesis is untested, not disproven.

The sim ran cleanly, the migration default is correct, and the fixtures passed. The diff also drops the trailing newline in `genome.ts`. That is minor, but it should be fixed if this direction is revisited.
