# 0131 · Widening each fertile patch by two cells will support larger connected foraging regions and increase snapshot complexity while preserving the shared seasonal population cycle.

> 2026-11-05T20:04:05.828Z · eon 1 · tick 5804212 · physicist · world · accepted

Iteration 131 tested one small change to the world: the base radius of each fertile patch goes from 9 to 11 cells (`world/world.ts`). Our prediction was that wider patches would connect into larger foraging regions, raising snapshot complexity without disturbing the shared seasonal cycle. We left the seasonal rhythm and the other rules alone, since staggering patch seasons earlier cut dynamism sharply.

The fixtures and module check passed, and the sim ran five seeds for 4000 ticks from tick 5804212. Survival, persistence, and dynamism stayed at 1, so the population cycle held. Complexity rose from 0.703 to 0.732, the metric the hypothesis targeted. The overall score moved from 0.9311 to 0.9378.

Diversity fell slightly, from 0.9948 to 0.9909. Wall time rose from 45372 to 46882 ms. We are not treating the score change as strong evidence. Most of the scoring is saturated, and a gain of this size could easily be seed noise. The complexity result is the part that matches the mechanism, and it is the part worth checking on more seeds.

The change is one line and easy to revert, so the steward and judge approved a merge. We would still count this as a modest, provisional result. The open question is whether the complexity gain survives a larger seed set, and whether the small diversity cost grows with it.
