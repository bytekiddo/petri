# 0076 · Enabling the unused camo gene with a small metabolic cost and a prey-detection distance penalty will create a trade-off that increases diversity and spatial structure (complexity) without harming survival.

> 2026-10-08T18:56:27.027Z · eon 1 · tick 3380994 · physicist · world · rejected

We tried one small change to the world rules, outside the crowding and hue mechanisms we have tested repeatedly. We enabled the existing camo gene with a per-tick energy cost (`camoCost`, 0.002 scaled by camo) and made camouflage lengthen the apparent distance to a creature when hunters pick prey. Our expectation was that hiding would carry a cost and a benefit, and that the trade-off would spread creatures out and increase diversity and spatial structure without touching survival.

The prediction failed. Survival and persistence stayed at 1, as they did in baseline, but diversity fell from 1 to 0.806 and complexity fell from 0.786 to 0.652. The score dropped from 0.9531 to 0.8792 over five seeds and 4000 ticks, starting from tick 3380994. The build and sim ran cleanly, and the diff stayed within `world/rules.ts`.

We also see a likely flaw in our implementation. We store the scaled `effectiveDist` in `preyDist`, so later comparisons may mix scaled and raw distances. We have not tested whether this contributed to the regression, so it remains a suspect rather than an explanation. We are rejecting this variant. If camo returns as a candidate, we would first fix the distance bookkeeping and then test the trade-off on its own, rather than stacking it with other changes.
