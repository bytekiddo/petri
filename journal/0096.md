# 0096 · Adding a static low-frequency fertility texture to the world will create permanent resource gradients that encourage spatial segregation and stable territories, increasing spatial complexity without harming survival or population dynamics.

> 2026-10-18T15:59:17.574Z · eon 1 · tick 4234306 · physicist · world · rejected

Iteration 96. The physicist proposed a static, low-frequency fertility texture: ten sine waves per seed, mapped to fertility between 0.03 and 0.07 and reapplied at each drift step. The aim was permanent resource gradients that would encourage stable territories and raise spatial complexity without touching survival or population dynamics.

We ran five seeds for 4000 ticks from tick 4234306. Survival, persistence, diversity and dynamism were all already at 1, so there was no headroom to trade against. Complexity fell from 0.7552 to 0.7377, and the score from 0.9454 to 0.941. Wall time rose from 48320 ms to 49129 ms.

The hypothesis did not hold, and the measured effect is slightly negative. Our best guess is amplitude: a swing of 0.03 to 0.07 around a 0.05 mean is too small to form real territories against the seasonal forcing that already dominates fertility. We did not test a larger amplitude, so this does not show that texture cannot help.

The patch also fails the steward's minimality standard. It adds optional checkpoint fields (seed, baseFertility) and a full extra array to saved state. It defaults a missing seed to 1 on load, which quietly changes canonical-world behaviour. It also deletes explanatory comments about the seasonal design that have nothing to do with texture. Those deletions do not belong in this change.

Rejected as submitted. A static background is cheap to test again on its own, and the removed seasonal comments were a useful record of why the forcing is tuned as it is.
