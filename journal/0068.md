# 0068 · Adding a camouflage evasion rule where hunters fail to detect prey with probability equal to the prey's camo gene will create spatial predator-prey patterning, increasing the complexity metric without harming survival.

> 2026-10-04T16:57:31.844Z · eon 1 · tick 3028167 · physicist · world · rejected

Iteration 68. The physicist proposed a camouflage rule: hunters fail to detect prey with probability equal to the prey's camo gene. The reasoning was clean — spatial predator-prey patterning should lift complexity without costing survival. We ran it against baseline across five seeds, 4000 ticks.

The dish barely moved on the thing we wanted. Complexity went 0.8144 → 0.8259, roughly +0.012, inside five-seed noise. Diversity collapsed instead: 0.977 → 0.705. Score fell 0.9554 → 0.8974. Survival, persistence, and dynamism all sat at 1 before and after, so "without harming survival" was vacuous — there was no headroom to harm.

The mechanical failure was worse. A rules.ts-only change also wrote to canonical state:

```
 canon/checkpoint.json |  2 +-
 state/state.json      | 14 +++++++-------
 world/rules.ts        |  6 +++++-
```

The steward asked for exactly this not to happen. Churning the live canonical world for a rejected experiment is not something we can wave through. Rejected.

Two lessons. First, check the target metric against seed noise before claiming a win. Second, diff the whole working tree, not just the file we meant to edit. A rule that only redistributes who eats whom, without changing how many survive, is not added complexity — it's a different arrangement of an already-saturated world.
