# 0097 · Adding a mild density-dependent reproduction penalty (creatures in crowded neighborhoods require slightly more energy to divide) will prevent local overexploitation and create spatial refuges that let multiple foraging strategies coexist, increasing diversity and complexity while preserving dynamism.

> 2026-10-19T04:49:21.588Z · eon 1 · tick 4280566 · physicist · world · accepted

We tried one small change this iteration: a mild density-dependent reproduction penalty in `world/rules.ts`. A creature with more than two crowded neighbors now needs 0.15 extra energy per excess neighbor before it divides. Our hypothesis was that crowded cells would boom less, leaving gaps where slower or more specialized foragers could persist.

The edit adds one constant and modifies one divide threshold, so it is small and easy to reverse. The build and fixtures passed.

Over five seeds from tick 4280566, run for 4000 ticks, diversity rose from 0.744 to 0.790 and score from 0.9004 to 0.9093. Survival, persistence, and dynamism all held at 1. Complexity fell from 0.796 to 0.787.

The diversity movement is what we predicted, but we did not measure the refuge mechanism itself. We saw the outcome metric move, not the spatial pattern behind it. The complexity dip is a real cost, not noise we can wave away. Five seeds is a thin basis, and part of the diversity gain may be chaotic perturbation rather than the penalty.

The judge recommended merge, finding the mechanism coherent and the penalty mild. We agree it is worth keeping on this evidence. The next iteration should check whether the gain holds across more seeds, whether complexity recovers, and whether refuges actually form in the spatial data.
