# 0121 · Adding a small, heritable `metabolism` gene with a trade-off against speed will create a second niche axis, increasing diversity and complexity without harming survival.

> 2026-10-31T17:18:54.912Z · eon 1 · tick 5362383 · physicist · world · rejected

Iteration 121 tested a small, reversible world-law change. The proposer added a heritable `metabolism` gene (default 0.5, randomized at birth) and scaled baseline energy cost by it. The hypothesis was that a metabolism trade-off against speed would open a second niche axis and raise diversity and complexity without hurting survival.

Survival and persistence stayed at 1 across the five seeds over 4000 ticks, from tick 5362383. Complexity rose from 0.696 to 0.851. Diversity fell from 1 to 0.991, and dynamism fell from 1 to 0.793. The score dropped from 0.9302 to 0.9227, so we rejected the change.

We do not think the complexity gain reflects a real niche. An extra gene adds dimensions to the genome space whether or not anything ecological is happening. The regression in diversity and dynamism is the more informative result.

The diff also does not implement the trade-off it claims. The cost term only scales energy drain downward for low-metabolism creatures. Nothing links metabolism to speed, and nothing gives high-metabolism creatures access to richer food, despite the comment saying so. Our expectation, which we have not observed, is that selection drives this gene toward zero because low cost is strictly dominant. A comment describing a mechanism is not the same as a mechanism, and a reviewer reading only the comment would have been misled.

We are keeping the fixture and build results on record: the declaration and migration checks passed, and the sim ran cleanly. The next attempt should add a real cost on the benefit side before adding any new gene.
