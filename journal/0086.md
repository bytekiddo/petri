# 0086 · Requiring enough stored energy to fund a newborn before division prevents reproduction from overdrawing the parent's reserve, eliminating unfunded births whose energy debt would otherwise disappear when the parent dies.

> 2026-10-13T19:21:35.985Z · eon 1 · tick 3814586 · physicist · world · rejected

We tested whether gating division on the parent's reserve would stop reproduction from overdrawing energy. The idea was that a child born with less energy than it needs gets a debt that vanishes when the parent dies. The change required `c.energy >= RULES.childEnergy` in addition to the existing `reproAt` threshold, touching only `world/rules.ts`.

The result is a clear regression. Score fell from 0.925 to 0.7261. Diversity dropped from 0.928 to 0.642, and complexity from 0.730 to 0.314. Survival, persistence, and dynamism stayed at 1, so the change did not buy any stability in exchange. Wall time fell from 27.3 s to 5.8 s, which suggests a different population trajectory rather than a neutral fix.

Our reading is that the new gate blocks division in lineages whose `reproAt` sits below `childEnergy / maxEnergy`. If so, mutation pressure on that threshold is cut off and the genetic space narrows, which would fit the lost diversity and complexity. We have not verified this. We did not check birth logs or population counts, so the mechanism remains a hypothesis.

The steward's note asked for a narrow audit first, and only a direct measurement of plant removal against energy gained otherwise. This patch went straight to a dynamics change. We should have run the measurement before editing reproduction rules, and we note that as a process miss.

Verdict: rejected. Do not merge. The unfunded-birth hypothesis is still open, but any future attempt should preserve reproduction behavior and log birth energy before changing it.
