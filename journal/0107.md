# 0107 · Increasing `RULES.foodPatches` from 7 to 8 adds another independently drifting fertile patch, which should raise spatial habitat heterogeneity and total carrying capacity, increasing the measured complexity while survival, persistence, and dynamism remain stable.

> 2026-10-24T16:04:32.130Z · eon 1 · tick 4753018 · physicist · world · rejected

We tested one change to the world rules in iteration 107: raising `RULES.foodPatches` from 7 to 8. The hypothesis was that an extra independently drifting fertile patch would add habitat heterogeneity and carrying capacity, raising complexity while leaving survival, persistence, and dynamism stable.

The result only half matched. Survival, persistence, and dynamism stayed at 1 in both runs. Complexity rose from 0.7212 to 0.7338, as predicted. Diversity fell from 0.9576 to 0.9125, which we did not predict. The composite score dropped from 0.9286 to 0.9229, so by the objective we are optimizing, the candidate is worse.

We think the complexity gain is too small to separate from noise across five seeds. The diversity drop is a clearer signal, and we do not yet have a mechanism for it. One guess is that the extra patch shifts where populations settle and reduces niche separation, but we have not checked that. The runs also took slightly longer, 47.1 seconds against 43.7, which is likely just the extra simulated patch and not a concern.

Population sat at the `maxPop` cap of 3000 in every branch. If the population never reaches the limit that extra carrying capacity would relieve, then the patch cannot help through that route, which fits the flat survival and persistence numbers.

We reject the change and revert the single constant. It is reversible and easy to re-test if a later change alters the population dynamics. Next we will keep to one small world-rule change at a time, with a stated ecological mechanism for each, and stop adding diagnostic scripts unless a result needs them.
