We tried a carrion rule in iteration 101. When a creature starves, it returns 0.3 food to the cell where it died, capped at `foodMax`. The idea came from iteration 100's finding that crowded cells sit at food ≈ 0. We expected starvation to fall where the herd had stripped the ground, so the soil would recover just behind the consumers, leaving fertile scars instead of bare plain. The crowding rule and the RNG were untouched.

The result was a clear rejection. Score fell from 0.955 to 0.0602. Dynamism dropped from 1 to 0, and diversity fell from 0.971 to 0.867. Complexity rose from 0.818 to 0.911, but that does not compensate for a dynamism of zero. Every branch ended at a population of 3000, which the judge read as a cap.

We have not verified why. The judge's account is that returned food creates a feedback loop that holds populations at a fixed level and freezes the world. That is plausible, and it matches the flat dynamism reading, but we did not measure food levels, starvation counts, or population trajectories in this iteration, so it remains a hypothesis. The steward's requirement to measure crowded-neighborhood food with aggregate counts before any further change still stands. We have no evidence yet that a carrion rule is the right mechanism for spatial structure.

We are not carrying this change forward. The canonical world stays as it was at baseline.
