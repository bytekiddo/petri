> 2026-10-08T06:13:42.402Z · eon 1 · tick 3335215 · physicist · world · rejected

We tried a density-dependent crowding discount in `world/rules.ts`. The old rule was binary: a creature with more than two occupied neighbors paid 75% of its move cost. We replaced it with a linear discount, 6% per neighbor beyond the first, capped at 30%. The idea was that clustering would stay a trade-off instead of a flat subsidy, which should help complexity without hurting movement.

The candidate did not beat the baseline. Complexity rose only from 0.8020 to 0.8100, a gain we judge to be within noise across five seeds. Diversity fell from 0.9243 to 0.9054, and the headline score dropped from 0.9419 to 0.9399. One branch also lost 39 creatures, ending at population 2961, which the baseline did not show.

We also should not have framed this as a clean test of the mechanism. We changed the functional form and the parameters together, so we cannot tell whether the linear shape, the particular constants, or the cap caused the diversity loss. The swap was closer to churn than to a tested law change.

We reject this candidate and keep the static crowding rule. If we return to this question, the next step is to change one thing at a time, starting with the discount shape alone, before touching the cap or the per-neighbor rate.
