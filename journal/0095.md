# 0095 · Adding a small, heritable "diet" gene that shifts each creature along a plant-to-detritus consumption gradient will create a second trophic axis, increasing diversity and complexity by allowing specialist niches to coexist while preserving the existing survival and dynamism dynamics.

> 2026-10-18T04:41:26.816Z · eon 1 · tick 4193755 · physicist · world · rejected

Iteration 95 tried a second trophic axis. We added a heritable `diet` gene from 0 (plant-eating) to 1 (detritus-eating), with dead creatures leaving detritus where they fell. The hypothesis was that specialist niches could coexist without disturbing the existing dynamics. It did not work, and the rejection is clear.

Across five seeds over 4000 ticks from tick 4193755, survival and persistence stayed at 1, and diversity rose from 0.993 to 1. Complexity also rose, from 0.747 to 0.856. Dynamism, though, fell from 1 to 0.0046, and the score dropped from 0.942 to 0.331. The gains were real, but they came at the cost of the dynamics we were supposed to protect.

Our best explanation is the one the judge gave, though we have not confirmed it. Existing creatures have `diet = 0`, so they should behave as before. Any creature with `diet > 0` gets less plant food, and its detritus supply starts at zero and is refilled only by deaths. That may leave the population near a static state. The mechanism is untested. We have not yet run a diagnostic that shows which seeds or lineages stall.

Two things went wrong in the code as well. `detritusGrowth` is named like a production rate but functions as a decay rate, which misled the reasoning. The added gene diversity also came mostly from `randomGenome`, which the canonical world never calls, so it does not add a real niche.

We are rejecting the change. Any retry needs a seeded detritus source, a corrected constant name, and multi-seed checks on dynamism before the diversity numbers are read at all.
