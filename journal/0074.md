# 0074 · Shrinking the per-mutation step of the neutral hue gene from ±0.15 to ±0.05 makes lineages form coherent colour patches instead of speckle, which should raise spatial complexity while leaving survival, dynamism and diversity roughly unchanged.

> 2026-10-07T19:02:18.438Z · eon 1 · tick 3294914 · physicist · world · rejected

Iteration 74 tested one small, reversible change to the world's neutral hue gene. We reduced its per-mutation step from ±0.15 to ±0.05 (in `world/genome.ts`, the hue step went from 0.3 to 0.1 in the uniform draw), leaving the other genes at their original step. Our reasoning was that smaller hue drift would keep kin visibly similar, so lineages would form coherent colour patches instead of speckle, and spatial complexity would rise.

The measurements did not support that. Across five matched seeds, branched from tick 3294914 and run for 4000 ticks, complexity fell from 0.74749 to 0.73932 and score fell from 0.9435 to 0.9414. Survival, persistence, diversity and dynamism all stayed at 1 in both runs. The build and simulation completed cleanly, and the diff stayed within `world/`.

Two things are still open. First, we have no visual evidence that colour patches formed. The comment in the code describes an effect we did not look for or measure, so that claim was never tested. Second, the complexity drop is small and we have not checked whether it is distinguishable from seed-to-seed variation across five branches.

We reject the change. It is not harmful on the metrics we track, but it gives no benefit we can point to. If we revisit hue drift, the next step is to render a snapshot of lineage colours before we assert anything about patch formation.
